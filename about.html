Same block, 54 years

A hi-fi shop that survived the CD era, the streaming era, and three economic catastrophes by doing one simple thing: never selling anything broken.

Gus Halvorsen opened Halcyon Stereo in the spring of 1974, in a former upholstery shop on Ankeny Street, with four shelves of receivers, a workbench he built himself, and a firm belief that a receiver should be repaired, not replaced. He was opinionated about capacitors. He was right about most things.

The shop grew the way good shops do: slowly, by reputation. Gus serviced the gear other stores wouldn’t touch. He taught half the stereo technicians in Portland how to set a cartridge. He kept the listening room open to anyone who wanted to sit and actually hear a system before buying it, which in 1978 was a radical idea and today is still rare enough to be one.

June Halvorsen took over in 2009 after twenty years at the bench. She kept every rule and added exactly one: every unit gets a written condition report, flaws and all, posted right on the tag. “Surprise is for birthday parties,” she says, “not for amplifiers.”

“A good receiver isn’t nostalgia. It’s engineering that was finished on the first try.”
— Gus Halvorsen, shop sign, 1974-present

The long version

Five decades, condensed

  • 1974

    Gus opens the doors

    Four shelves of receivers, one workbench, and the first customer, a violinist who stayed for three hours listening to two speakers she could not afford. She bought them anyway. She still comes in.

  • 1983

    The CD scare

    Half the industry said analog was finished. Gus rented the vacant storefront next door and doubled his service capacity instead. The bet paid for the whole block by 1986.

  • 1998

    The listening room

    The upstairs office became a proper listening room: eleven seats, acoustic panels June’s mother upholstered, and the big system that has anchored every Friday night since.

  • 2009

    June takes over

    Second-generation ownership. Written condition reports become policy, the trade-in desk opens, and the first Saturday clinic runs out of seats in an hour.

  • 2026

    The long boom

    Vintage hi-fi is mainstream again and the bench is busier than it has ever been. We are hiring a third technician and the waiting list for tube amps is honest about being a waiting list.
